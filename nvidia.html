NVIDIA

Equity Research Report · August 4, 2026

NVIDIA's investment case centers on AI infrastructure, its position in the AI accelerator market, CUDA switching costs, future inference demand, and opportunities in autonomous machines and robotics.

Business

NVIDIA sells enterprise and data center hardware, consumer hardware, enterprise software platforms, and cloud and digital services. It generates most of its revenue from hardware and software platforms for AI and data centers.

Growth Thesis

The report identifies autonomous machines and robotics, the shift from AI training toward inference, agentic workflows, and increasing AI integration as potential long-term growth drivers.

Strengths

  • CUDA and AI hardware help support NVIDIA's position in the AI accelerator market.
  • High switching costs and CUDA codebases can make it difficult for customers to switch.
  • Continued development of AI computing technologies can help NVIDIA remain competitive as workloads grow.

Risks

  • Heavy reliance on TSMC and Taiwan-based manufacturing creates geopolitical and supply-chain risk.
  • Slower AI demand or adoption could reduce infrastructure spending and chip demand.
  • If NVIDIA hardware does not meet future customer expectations, alternatives could become more attractive.

AI Summary

NVIDIA is a leading semiconductor and artificial intelligence company that generates most of its revenue from AI accelerators, data center hardware, software platforms, and related computing technologies. Its growth is supported by the continued expansion of AI infrastructure, its position in the AI accelerator market, and high switching costs created by its CUDA platform. Its main risks include reliance on TSMC, potential slowdowns in AI infrastructure spending, and the possibility of customers shifting toward alternative technologies.

Final Verdict

NVIDIA is viewed in the report as an attractive long-term investment because it can benefit from continued AI infrastructure expansion while serving companies competing in the AI race. The largest counterweight is manufacturing concentration in Taiwan, particularly the potential impact of a low-probability blockade.
