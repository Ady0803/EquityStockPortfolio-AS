NVIDIA

Equity Research Report · August 4, 2026

Business Overview

  • Enterprise and Data Center Hardware: AI Accelerators and GPUs, Central Processing Units (CPUs), AI Supercomputers and Servers, High Speed Networking
  • Consumer Hardware: Gaming Graphics Cards, RTX AI PCs and Workstations
  • Enterprise Software Platforms: CUDA Platform, NVIDIA AI Enterprise Suite, NVIDIA Omniverse, Automotive Software (Drive)
  • Cloud and Digital Services: DGX Cloud, NVIDIA Cloud Services, GeForce NOW

How the Company Makes Money

NVIDIA makes the majority of its revenue by selling hardware and software platforms for AI and data centers. The company operates under a profitable “fabless: business model, where it designs its technology in-house but outsourced physical manufacturing to third-party semiconductor foundries. Aside from this, the company sells desktop and laptop graphic cards under the GeForce RTX banner directly to PC gamers and hardware enthusiasts, this represents its traditional core business. They also design high-end professional workstations and workstation GPUs, which generate revenue through hardware sales to professionals and businesses that require high-performance computing. The rest of its revenue comes from selling complete system-on-a-chip microprocessors, custom silicon components, and other hardware to companies, which can then incorporate these products into their own systems.

My Investment Opinion

My investment rating on NVIDIA is 9/10. I would invest in this company because it benefits from the AI infrastructure expansion as companies competing in the AI race need AI accelerators/GPUs, with NVIDIA currently controlling a majority of the AI accelerator market. Pair this with the company’s high switching costs, which help it maintain customer retention and recurring revenue, and this makes NVIDIA a highly attractive investment. The company relies heavily on manufacturing partners in Taiwan, particularly TSMC. Escalating military tensions could result in a blockade that would hinder NVIDIA’s ability to provide chips, putting downward pressure on its stock. While NVIDIA is working to diversify the geographic locations where it sources its hardware, the fact that the majority of manufacturing is still done in Taiwan is what makes it difficult to give it a higher score. The chance of a blockade is low; however, the consequences that come with it outweigh the low probability of a blockade. Overall, however, the company’s main strengths help offset the low-probability risk, making this company an attractive investment.

AI Summary

NVIDIA is a leading semiconductor and artificial intelligence company that generates most of its revenue from AI accelerators, data center hardware, software platforms, and related computing technologies. Its growth is supported by the continued expansion of AI infrastructure, its dominant position in the AI accelerator market, and high switching costs created by its CUDA platform. NVIDIA also has opportunities to expand into areas such as autonomous machines, robotics, and AI inference as AI becomes more widely integrated into everyday technology. Its main risks include its heavy reliance on TSMC for chip manufacturing, potential slowdowns in AI infrastructure spending, and the possibility that customers could shift toward alternative technologies if NVIDIA’s hardware does not meet future demands.

Strengths & Risks

  • Strength: NVIDIA’s CUDA platform, AI hardware performances, and relationships with long-standing cloud providers allow it to establish a dominant position in the AI accelerator market, which in turn lets it capitalize on the AI boom
  • Strength: High switching costs and CUDA codebases make it difficult for customers to switch away and allow NVIDIA to have pricing power
  • Strength: NVIDIA’s continued development of increasingly powerful AI computing technologies allows it to remain competitive as AI workloads continues to grow
  • Risk: NVIDIA relies heavily on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) to manufacture its chips. As escalating military tensions between China and Taiwan represent a threat, any blockade could severely hinder business and disrupt the semiconductor industry, potentially delaying the AI infrastructure expansion and ROI until resolved. This could put downward pressure on NVIDIA’s stock.
  • Risk: Companies participating in the AI infrastructure expansion could slow their spending due to lower AI demand and slower AI adoption. This could lead to lower demand for chips and hardware, slowing revenue growth. This could in turn into lower-than-expected returns for investors
  • Risk: If AI infrastructure demand evolves in a way that NVIDIA GPU clusters and hardware cannot meet customer expectations, companies could begin considering alternatives and shifting away from NVIDIA. This could put downward pressure on its stock, potentially slowing or reducing investor returns. However, NVIDIA’s high switching costs provide a barrier to companies switching away, giving the company additional time to respond and catch up to market demand.

Growth Trends

A few trends that could help NVIDIA Corporation grow over the next 5–10 years include entering new markets that its leadership believes could become the next multi-trillion dollar opportunities. The company believes that instead of only powering software bots they should provide the brains for physical autonomous machines. This is a trend that could help the company grow as factories globally are looking to automate work in order to deal with labor shortages. As such, millions of robots could require localized chips and simulation software that NVIDIA can work to provide. The second trend that could help NVIDIA grow is the pivot from AI “Training” to “Inference”. In the early years of the AI boom, the industry was dominated by the "training" of large AI models, which required massive one-time purchases. In other words, these purchases would not provide the company selling the hardware with reliable recurring income. However, based on the increasing incorporation of AI today, the next decade could be increasingly defined by inference, which can be explained as the constant running and processing of those models. As billions of users adopt agentic workflows and smartphones get more and more AI-integrated, the computing power and hardware required to run AI could dwarf what was required for training. The growth driver therefore becomes inference, which demands continuous and stable computing capacity. This ensures that even after tech giants finish building their huge AI data centers, they still need to buy chips to process daily consumer traffic.

About the Company: Competitors

NVIDIA competes with various technology companies depending on the industry. However, unlike Coca Cola and Pepsi, it has no single competitor that matches it across all its markets. Its biggest competitors in each industry include:

  • AI and Data Center Hardware: AMD (Advanced Micro Devices), Intel, In-House Cloud Hyperscalers
  • Data Center Networking and Infrastructure: Broadcom, Cisco Systems and Marvell Technology
  • Consumer Gaming and PC Hardware: AMD, Intel
  • Autonomous Vehicles and Automotive Systems: Qualcomm, Mobileye, Tesla

Growth Story

Investors are interested in this company because it benefits from the global artificial intelligence boom regardless of who wins. This is largely due to NVIDIA’s dominant position in the AI accelerator market, where it holds an estimated 75-81% market share right now. Major technology companies such as Microsoft, Meta, Google, and Amazon purchase the parts and hardware necessary from NVIDIA, allowing the company to benefit from the AI regardless of which company ultimately leads it. On top of this, the company has high switching costs as companies build software and train engineers around NVIDIA’s CUDA platform. As a result if companies want to switch to competitors they would have to spend millions of dollars and rewrite their whole software code bases. This gives NVIDIA pricing power and high customer retention. To add to this, similar to Microsoft, NVIDIA has an immense backlog that stretches deep into the future, amounting to about 500 billion dollars.

Final Verdict

Overall, NVIDIA is an attractive long-term investment. It has low-moderate debt risk because, based on the financial data I analyzed, NVIDIA's cash and cash equivalents were only somewhat higher than its total debt. However, its debt should also be evaluated in terms of its strong cash flow and liquidity, which help balance it out. If NVIDIA maintains this trend, its debt should remain fairly easy to manage, making it a low-to-moderate financial risk. As explained in my investment opinion, the company relies heavily on manufacturing partners in Taiwan, particularly TSMC. A low-probability blockade could heavily impact NVIDIA’s stock, delaying ROI until the situation is resolved and potentially lowering it overall. However, the company is working on diversifying where it sources its hardware from, which means this issue could get addressed with time. In the meantime, while the threat remains significant, the low probability of a blockade and NVIDIA’s strengths outweigh this risk. In today’s world, corporate titans are competing in the AI race, and they all need somewhere to obtain their AI accelerators and GPUs. As it stands right now the majority of the AI accelerator market falls to NVIDIA. Because of this, NVIDIA stands to benefit regardless of which companies come out on top in the race, as long as it maintains a majority of the market. In this AI accelerator market, NVIDIA’s biggest competitor is AMD. However, NVIDIA currently maintains a dominant position in the market, which supports my view that the company is well positioned to benefit from continued AI infrastructure expansion. Of course there are other risks and strengths that come along with the investment, but these are the biggest ones.
