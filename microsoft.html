Microsoft

Equity Research Report · July 29, 2026

Business Overview

Microsoft sells a variety of products from different categories such as:

Consumer Software and Subscriptions: Windows 11, Microsoft 365 Personal/Family, Xbox Game Pass, Microsoft Copilot Pro

Hardware and Devices: Surface Laptops and 2-in-1s, Xbox Series X and Series S, PC Accessories

Enterprise and Business Software: Microsoft 365 Enterprise, Microsoft Teams, Dynamics 365, Power Platform

Cloud and Developer Services: Microsoft Azure, Github, Visual Studio

How It Makes Money

Microsoft makes money primarily through its cloud computing infrastructure and digital business software. The business operates in a connected ecosystem where enterprise cloud storage, workplace productivity software, and artificial intelligence fuel each other's growth. Microsoft makes 40% of its total revenue from Intelligent Cloud including Microsoft Azure, and Enterprise Services and Server Licenses. It also generates highly recurring revenue from productivity and business processes such as Microsoft 365, LinkedIn, and Dynamics 365. It also earns additional revenue each year from personal computing like video gaming, Windows OEM Licenses, and search/news advertising.

My Investment Opinion

My investment rating on Microsoft is 9/10. I would invest in this company because, based on current trends, I believe it has strong long-term growth potential with manageable risks. Although Microsoft's heavy investment in artificial intelligence may take longer than expected to yield results, I believe the company's leadership in cloud computing and artificial intelligence provides it with significant opportunities for future growth. Because of this, I believe it to be a more attractive long-term investment than Apple. This does not mean Apple is a poor investment; rather, I believe Microsoft currently offers greater long-term growth potential.

AI Summary

Microsoft is a global technology company that provides software, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, enterprise technology, and gaming products. Its long-term growth is supported by expanding demand for cloud services, artificial intelligence, and its ability to offer integrated business solutions that encourage customers to remain within the Microsoft ecosystem. The company's strong financial position, recurring revenue, and leadership in cloud computing and AI further strengthen its outlook. Its main risks include continued price increases that could encourage some customers to consider lower-cost alternatives, uncertainty surrounding the pace of AI adoption, and intense competition from other major technology companies.

Strengths & Risks

The company is financially strong, with cash and short-term investments nearly double its total debt, making its debt highly manageable.

Microsoft's leadership in cloud computing and artificial intelligence provides strong long-term growth opportunities

High switching costs make it difficult for many of Microsoft's enterprise customers to move away from its products and services, supporting long-term customer retention and stable recurring revenue

Risks

Continued price increases could encourage customers to consider lower-cost alternatives, potentially slowing customer growth and in turn increasing competitive pressure

The company's heavy investment in artificial intelligence could lead to slower returns if the adoption of AI grows more slowly than expected.

Microsoft is competing against some of the world’s largest companies in cloud computing and artificial intelligence. This could lead to slower growth and reduce returns on its investments

Growth Trends

The trends Microsoft is following right now that could help it grow over the next 5-10 years include the fact that Microsoft can charge more from its existing customers as they upgrade their technology while the cost of serving those customers can decrease as technology becomes more efficient. Besides, technology is transitioning from basic chatbots where you have to feed them the prompt to autonomous AI agents that can complete everything in the background. And Microsoft owns Copilot Studio, the platform enterprises use to build these agents. Instead of charging a subscription fee per human, which was the norm before, Microsoft can potentially take advantage of this trend by charging enterprises based on the amount of work and computing power their agents use. This makes the system highly scalable and opens a brand-new stream of revenue. Another trend that helps the company grow is the enterprise capturing market share from smaller and more specialized software competitors. As companies are actively reducing the number of independent software vendors they rely on in order to avoid numerous headaches, they are shifting to Microsoft. This is due to the fact that Microsoft is the best “all-in-one” enterprise for companies. Instead of having to pay numerous bills to platforms like Zoom, Slack, and Okta, they can consolidate entirely onto Microsoft and use its platforms like Microsoft Teams Phone and Microsoft Defender. On top of all this, Microsoft's $678 billion commercial remaining performance obligations provide testimony to at least two years of strong revenue visibility.

Growth Story

Investors are interested in this company because it is one of the world’s leading enterprise software companies while simultaneously having one of the world’s largest cloud computing and artificial intelligence businesses. Its Cloud and AI growth engine revenue is expanding rapidly, with its cloud revenue surpassing the $100 billion annual milestone. The company has also successfully been selling compute power and its Microsoft 365 Copilot software alone has surged past 30 million corporate seats. Microsoft also has a revenue backlog totaling $678 billion that provides strong revenue visibility and reduces economic uncertainty. On top of this, Microsoft has one other underlying factor that makes investors interested. This factor is the fact that once a company switches its IT architecture to Microsoft, switching to a different architecture is not only costly but has other technical risks. This effectively supports long-term customer retention.

Final Verdict

Overall, Microsoft appears to be a financially strong company with manageable debt. Its cash and short-term investments are nearly double its total debt, making it an attractive long-term investment. Its leadership in cloud computing and artificial intelligence provides strong long-term growth opportunities. In addition, its high switching costs support recurring revenue and strong long-term customer retention. Microsoft faces increasing competition from rivals, along with the risk of customer resistance to price increases, and delayed returns on artificial intelligence investments. Even so, I believe its strengths far outweigh its risks and make it one of the strongest long-term investments in my portfolio.
